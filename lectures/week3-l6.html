Normal / Gaussian Distribution

A symmetric, bell-shaped theoretical distribution.

It is defined by only two parameters:

μ = 0.0
σ = 1.0

For a Normal distribution: skewness = 0 and kurtosis = 0.

Approximately 68% lies within μ ± 1σ.

Approximately 95% lies within μ ± 2σ.

Approximately 99.7% lies within μ ± 3σ.

f(x) = 1 σ√2π exp [− (x − μ)² 2σ² ]
For reference only: you do not need to calculate this by hand.

Standardise Data

Different gaussian distributions can have different means and standard deviations.

Standardisation puts them onto the same scale:

Z = X − mean std

After standardisation: Z ~ N(0, 1).

This notion means the standardised variable Z follows a standard normal distribution with a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1.

Guess: Has This Time Series Been Standardised?

Is this the original data, or the standardised data?

Clue: standardisation changes the centre and spread, but it does notchange the shape of a time series or force the data to be Gaussian.

Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

When many independent contributions from the same distribution are added or averaged, which we call iid: (independent and identically distributed), their distribution tends towards a Normal distribution, even when the original data are not Normal.

Where Could That Distance Come From?

1. A real mismatch: the data do not follow a Normal distribution.
2. Randomness: even samples drawn from a Normal distribution do not look exactly identical.

Hypothesis Testing: Lilliefors Test

You have just completed a full hypothesis-testing workflow for Normality.

1. Define H₀ and H₁ 2. Define a distance 3. Simulate randomness 4. Set a threshold 5. Compare OBS data

Null hypothesis (H₀):the data follow a Normal distribution.

Alternative hypothesis (H₁):the data do not follow a Normal distribution.

D is the maximum distance between the empirical CDF of standardised data and the theoretical Normal CDF.

from statsmodels.stats.diagnostic import lilliefors
D, p_value = lilliefors(data, dist="norm")

p-value: if H₀ were true, the probability of obtaining a distance this large or more extreme.

Set significance level (α) = 0.05.
This is an arbitrary convention, not a truth or a golden standard.

p-value < 0.05
Reject H₀: the difference is statistically significant.

VS

p-value ≥ 0.05
Do not reject H₀: the evidence is not strong enough.
